What is Elo?

Elo is a rating system first used for chess. Every team carries a number, and every result moves points from the loser to the winner. Beating a stronger team earns more than beating a weaker one, and losing to a weaker team costs more.

Before each match the two ratings give an expected result. A team rated 100 points higher than its opponent is expected to take about 64% of the points; 200 points higher, about 76%. After the match, each team moves by the gap between what it got and what was expected.

  • Starting point: 1500, the average of a division.
  • Step size: 20 points times the gap between result and expectation, so a shock win moves ratings more than an expected one.
  • Winning margin: a two-goal win counts 1.5 times, and bigger wins a little more again.
  • Home advantage: the home team is treated as 60 points stronger when working out the expectation.
  • Each summer: ratings are pulled 20% of the way back towards 1500, since squads change.

Across divisions. Each division keeps its own scale centred on 1500, so a 1600 in the Championship is not a 1600 in the Premier League. When a team is promoted or relegated, it carries its rating across, adjusted by how much earlier teams making the same move gained or lost. That's why this list ranks one division at a time.
